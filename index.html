Grok

A small network learns (a + b) mod 97 from 30% of the possible pairs. It memorises its training examples within 400 steps and keeps failing on unseen pairs for tens of thousands more. Then, around step 30,000, it suddenly generalises. Inside, it has built an algorithm out of waves: each neuron stores a number as an angle, and the network adds numbers by rotating. This lab lets you watch that algorithm form.

Inside the weights: noise becomes waves

Left: the input weights of all 256 hidden neurons (rows) for each value of a (columns), sorted by the frequency each neuron ends up using. Right: the same neurons in frequency space (a discrete Fourier transform of each row). At initialisation both are noise. By the end, every neuron is a clean wave at a single frequency, and together they cover all 48 frequencies. This "purity" rises steadily long before test accuracy moves. That makes it a progress measure: it shows the capability forming before the capability shows up in the test score.

Open up one neuron

Pick a neuron. Its input weights trace a cosine wave at frequency k. Because the same neuron receives both a and b, its activation over the whole a × b grid forms diagonal stripes. It depends only on a + b, which is exactly the quantity the task needs. Neurons at the same frequency with different phases combine, through cos(x)cos(y) − sin(x)sin(y) = cos(x + y), into something like a rotation on a circle of 97 positions.

05

Why a toy like this matters for AI safety

Sudden capabilities

Large models sometimes gain abilities abruptly as they grow or train longer. Grokking is a small, fully observable case of that jump, where you can see the cause.

Progress measures

Wave purity rose for 25,000 steps while test accuracy sat near zero. Measures read from inside the model can warn of a capability before behavioural evaluations detect it.

Verification by intervention

Reading an algorithm off the weights is a hypothesis. Ablations test it causally. That is the standard interpretability work is held to when it is used to audit models.

Method & sources

Model and training

One-hidden-layer MLP with 256 ReLU neurons. The input is one-hot a and one-hot b (97 each), with separate embedding matrices Wa, Wb summed into the hidden layer, and a linear readout W2 to 97 logits. Written from scratch in NumPy, with full-batch AdamW (lr 10⁻³, β = 0.9 / 0.98, weight decay 1.0) for 40,000 steps on a random 30% of the 9,409 pairs. Strong weight decay drives the network toward the lighter, general solution. No GPU needed.

What is stored

Train and test loss and accuracy every 100 steps. 73 checkpoints, spaced logarithmically early and every 1,000 steps later, each with Wa (int8) and a bit-packed correctness grid over all 9,409 pairs. The final weights are int8 for the live calculator. The final network gets 98.8% of all pairs right, 98.3% on held-out pairs.

Wave purity

For each neuron, the share of its Wa Fourier power (frequencies 1–48) in its single strongest frequency, reported as the median over neurons. A random vector scores about 0.09.

Honest caveats

This is an MLP, not the one-layer transformer in Nanda et al., so the mechanism is closer to Gromov's analysis. Neurons here spread over all frequencies instead of settling on a handful of "key" ones. The qualitative story is the same: memorise, form a circuit, clean up, and generalise suddenly.
